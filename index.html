The artist behind Adaaye

A childhood love, painted into a life.

My journey with art began in childhood, with little scribbles at home and a heart that found joy in the colours, patterns and stories of traditional Indian art, especially Madhubani. What began as an innocent love slowly blossomed into a self-taught journey of discovering, learning and creating.

Over time, art became more than just a passion. It became a way of expressing myself and staying connected to my roots. Adaaye was born from this love: a dream to celebrate the timeless beauty of Indian traditions while embracing the simplicity and elegance of modern aesthetics.

Every creation is a reflection of my journey, where age-old art forms meet contemporary expression, and every little detail carries a story, a memory and a piece of my heart.

To me, being Artistically Indian is about honouring where we come from while allowing our traditions to evolve, finding beauty in our heritage and giving it a voice of its own through art.

— Titas Basu

Titas Basu's journey.

From little scribbles at home to a self-taught practice in Madhubani and Indian folk art. Here's where it began, and where it's led.

  1. Early years

    Little scribbles at home

    It started in childhood: scribbles at home, and a heart drawn to the colours, patterns and stories of traditional Indian art, especially Madhubani. Nobody called it art at the time. It was simply what made her happiest.

  2. Learning the craft

    Self-taught, one brushstroke at a time

    With no formal training, Titas taught herself: the fine double-line work Madhubani is known for, mixing colours by eye, and the folk traditions of India alongside it. Many canvases were painted before the first one felt right.

  3. A turning point

    The first commission

    A friend asked for a hand-painted gift instead of something bought. That small piece was the first anyone paid for, and it raised a question Titas hadn't asked herself yet: could this be more than a passion?

  4. Founding Adaaye

    Giving the dream a name

    Adaaye began as a small Instagram page, a place to share finished pieces and take a handful of orders: one artist, a brush, and a dream to give Indian art a voice of its own. The brand keeps its birthday on 13 August, the same day as Titas's own.

  5. Today

    Adaaye, now online

    Launched on Mahalaya, adaaye.in brings hand-painted coasters, trays, wall art, kurtas and keepsakes to homes across India. Every piece is still painted by hand by Titas herself, and nothing is ever printed.

Meet the artist and the studio cat

Where Adaaye begins: a workshop by the water in rural West Bengal, where potters, weavers and painters still work the way their families always have.

Four stages, one brush.

Nothing at Adaaye is machine-printed. Every commission moves through the same four stages a Madhubani artist has always used. Only the subject changes.

  1. Sketch the outline

    The subject, whether a peacock, a lotus or a family crest, is drawn freehand in pencil, double-lined the way Madhubani work always begins.

  2. Mix the pigment

    Acrylic is hand-mixed to match the earth reds, indigos and turmeric yellows traditional to Bengal folk colour.

  3. Fill the motif

    Fine double lines are filled in with cross-hatch and dot work, the signature texture of Madhubani art.

  4. Seal and finish

    A protective seal is added so the piece, be it canvas, coaster or keepsake, is ready for daily life at home.
